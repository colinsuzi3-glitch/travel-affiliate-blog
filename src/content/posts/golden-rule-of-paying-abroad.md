If you’ve ever handed over your Canadian or American credit card at a café in Europe or a beachside restaurant in Brazil, you’ve probably seen that sneaky question pop up on the handheld payment terminal: Would you like to pay in CAD/USD or the local currency?

It feels helpful—like the machine is doing the math for you so you know exactly what your dinner costs back home. Don’t fall for it.

When you choose your home currency, you’re agreeing to something called Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC). Instead of letting Visa, Mastercard, or your own bank convert the charge at the near-spot exchange rate, you’re allowing the merchant’s payment processor to invent its own exchange rate—quietly tacking on a 3% to 12% markup for the "convenience." Worse still, many North American banks will still hit you with their standard 2.5% foreign transaction fee on top of it, simply because the transaction happened overseas.

**How to beat it every time:**

- Always tap the local currency (BRL, EUR, MXN, etc.) on the screen.
- Watch the wording: Some terminals try to nudge you into the worse rate with a big green button that says "Accept Conversion." Always hit "Decline Conversion" or "Pay Without Conversion."
- Use the same rule at ATMs: If a cash machine offers to lock in a "guaranteed" exchange rate in your home currency, decline it and let your own bank handle the math.

**This happened to me.** On one of my trips in South America, I always chose to pay in Canadian currency, until I realized I was paying huge conversion fees to the owner of the payment machine. At first I thought it was a hidden service charge. Later I found out it was their sneaky way of charging a fee to convert my Canadian dollars into the local currency. So beware.

It takes two seconds at the counter, requires zero extra effort, and easily saves you the cost of a few extra caipirinhas—or a nice dinner—over the course of a trip.
